Composition of Mavita Health

The Mavita Health mineral complex contains the following ingredients:

Silicon

Silicon can be found in the connective tissues, hair and nails. This substance is not really present in our current nutrition. Several symptoms in the absence of silicon are weak and brittle nails (which break immediately), dry and dull hair and wrinkles. Wrinkles can arise because this mineral is needed  with the formation of connective tissues, which support the skin from the inside. 

Sources of silicon are: whole grains, vegetables, fiber, citrus and hard drinking water.

Selenium

A great number of people has chronic shortage of selenium. Selenium occurs in the enzyme glutathione peroxidase, which has a strong anti-oxidant effect. Furthermore, selenium also assists in regulating the blood pressure, it increases the resistance and helps with the removal of toxic metals from the body. It also plays a role with the formation of coenzyme Q10, which is involved with converting food into energy. 

Sources of selenium are: whole meal bread, garlic, nuts, bran, vegetables, organ meats, milk and fish.

Magnesium

Magnesium is also called the anti-stress mineral. This mineral is an important nutrient for the brain. It increases resistance to stress, depression, tension and prevents mental fatigue. Magnesium also enhances memory and concentration. Furthermore, magnesium is involved in the release of energy from food and the proper functioning of the nervous system and muscles. Finally, this mineral is involved in the formation of strong teeth and bones, and is employed as the co-factor of B and C vitamins.

Sources of magnesium are: green leafy vegetables, nuts, brown rice, soybeans, whole grains, legumes and fish.

Calcium

Calcium is the mineral that is in the greatest proportion (about 1.5 kg) available in the body. Of the total amount, 99% is present in the bones and teeth. Calcium is one of the main constituents of the skeleton. The remaining part is in the organs, blood and muscles

Calcium is stored in the muscle cells in certain organelles. When  a nerve impulse is send from the brain to a muscle, calcium ions are released. This tightens the muscle itself. The mineral is essential for proper heart functioning and plays a role in the transmission of the nerve impulse. Calcium also has a regulatory effect on blood pressure and is involved in blood coagulation processes.

Nutrients rich in calcium include green vegetables, cheese, milk, dairy products, fish, nuts and hard tap water.

Zinc

Zinc plays a role in hundreds of biological processes in the body. It is, amongst others, involved in the synthesis of DNA, RNA, and hormones (testosterone). The mineral is also involved in the vitamin A function and in the metabolism of carbohydrates, alcohol and fatty acids. Zinc acts as a coenzyme of the enzyme superoxide dismutase (SOD), which protects the body against free radicals. Zinc is also important for the immune system. Symptoms of low zinc levels are: reduced resistance, hair loss, fatigue, white spots on the nails, irritability, skin problems and poor appetite.

Sources of zinc are: oysters, fish, (organ)-meats, vegetables, mushrooms, whole grain products, milk, egg yolk and yeast.

Taurine

Taurine is the most abundant amino acid in the human body, it plays an important role in the metabolism of man. Taurine gives energy and is important for a good cardiovascular health. The amino acid is of particular importance in electrically active tissues, and is for that reason, primarily found at the level of the central-nervous system, skeletal muscles, heart, and the brain.

Taurine is mainly found in protein rich foods such as meat, fish, shellfish, poultry and eggs.

MSM

MSM (Methylsulfonylmethane) is a form of organically bound sulfur.

The therapeutic effect of sulfur has long been known. Already in antiquity was bathing in the sulfur-rich hot springs, often of volcanic origin, a popular method to get rid of pain, especially rheumatic complaints. In many modern spas, the water often is rich in sulfur compounds.

MSM is also found in food, but in very small quantities and many is lost in the cooking process. Many people unnoticeably appear to have a shortage of MSM therefore. As the age increases, the amount of MSM and sulfur in the body deteriorates. The many body processes in which sulfur is involved, then function less well, which eventually results in many aging phenomena.
